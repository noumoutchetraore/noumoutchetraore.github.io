Transition to a Knowledge-Based Economy in Côte d'Ivoire: Panel Data Analysis

Master's thesis, Anadolu University, July 2026. Supervisor: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Burak Tuğberk Tosunoğlu

Available at the National Thesis Center (Turkish title copied on click: paste it in the search field).

Abstract

This thesis examines the relationship between the knowledge economy and economic growth in ECOWAS countries, and the policy implications of this relationship for Côte d'Ivoire, using an indicator set adapted from the four-pillar structure of the World Bank's Knowledge Assessment Methodology (KAM). It combines a Basic Scorecard analysis with short-run panel data estimations over 2010 to 2022. The results show that the reference group has a higher knowledge economy performance than ECOWAS and Côte d'Ivoire, although Côte d'Ivoire recorded significant relative progress over the period. Panel estimates indicate that capital accumulation is consistently associated with growth, while among knowledge economy indicators only education expenditure, medium- and high-technology exports, and mobile subscriptions are significant. The findings point to the importance of strengthening education, institutional capacity and the innovation ecosystem.

Working paper (unpublished)

Does Protection Educate Industry? Tariffs, Productive Power, and Industrialisation in Developing Countries

Manuscript available upon request

Abstract

Motivated by Friedrich List's theory of productive forces, this paper constructs an Index of Productive Power (IPP) through principal component analysis of four dimensions of productive capacity: energy infrastructure, physical capital formation, human capital, and institutional quality. Using a balanced panel of 27 developing and emerging economies over 2000 to 2022, it applies the Hansen (1999) panel threshold model with the IPP as the threshold variable. The results show that the effect of tariff protection on manufacturing value added per capita differs substantially across three productive capacity regimes: insignificant in the low-capacity regime, negative and significant in the intermediate regime, and large and positive in the high-capacity regime. These findings suggest that the effectiveness of trade policy is conditional on the stage of productive development.
